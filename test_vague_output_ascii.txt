Query: What is pre-emption in Pakistani law?
----------------------------------------
Answer:
Pre-emption in Pakistani law refers to the right of a person to purchase a property in preference to others, typically in cases where the property is being sold. This right is governed by the Pre-emption Act, 1990, and is applicable to agricultural land, as well as other types of properties in certain circumstances. The concept of pre-emption is based on the idea that a person who has a prior interest in a property, such as a neighbor or a co-sharer, should have the first opportunity to purchase the property if it is being sold.

According to the Pre-emption Act, 1990, a person who has a right of pre-emption can exercise this right if they are a co-sharer in the property, a neighbor, or a tenant of the property. The right of pre-emption can be exercised in cases where the property is being sold, gifted, or otherwise transferred. The person exercising the right of pre-emption must do so within a specified period, typically 30 days, from the date of the sale or transfer. If the person exercising the right of pre-emption is unable to pay the full purchase price, they may be required to deposit a portion of the price and provide security for the remaining amount.

It is worth noting that the right of pre-emption is not absolute and can be restricted or excluded in certain circumstances. For example, if the property is being sold to a person who is not a citizen of Pakistan, the right of pre-emption may not apply. Additionally, if the property is being sold through a court auction, the right of pre-emption may be limited or excluded. In cases where the right of pre-emption is disputed, the matter may be taken to court for resolution. The court will consider the specific circumstances of the case and apply the relevant provisions of the Pre-emption Act, 1990, to determine whether the right of pre-emption has been validly exercised.
